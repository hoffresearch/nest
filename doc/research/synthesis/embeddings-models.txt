================================================================================================
synthesis :: embeddings-models
embedding and representation models (text + image, including tiny/static)
================================================================================================

state of the art and concrete nest recommendations for this category.

summary:     The embeddings-models corpus splits cleanly into two layers for nest, which only consumes float
             outputs (never runs inference): (1) a build-side production layer (CLIP,
             BERT/WordPiece, sentence-transformers, BGE-M3, Qwen3-Embedding, Model2Vec, SetFit,
             rust-sbert, rust-bert, word2vec, GloVe) that pins the post-encoder convention and
             ships cheap deterministic encoders; and (2) a storage/scoring layer that is the
             real prize for the 'compress more than everyone' north star. The collective state
             of the art is converging on one contract that nest already half-implements: produce
             L2-normalized unit vectors so cosine == dot product (CLIP, word2vec, all ST
             models), then aggressively compress the embeddings section along FOUR orthogonal,
             multiplicative axes -- value precision (f16/int8, already in nest), dimensionality
             (Matryoshka truncate-then-renormalize: Qwen3/BGE/ST), sub-byte vector quantization
             (fastText product quantization, ~8x), and representation sparsity (SPLADE/BGE-M3
             learned-sparse, ~99% zeros, zstd-friendly) -- with quality guarded by exactly
             nest's existing pattern: a lossy form for candidate generation plus a mandatory
             exact-cosine rerank that makes the returned score honest. nest's int8 'tiny' preset
             (0.283 ratio, recall@10 0.992) is mid-pack; PQ + MRL + sparse stack
             multiplicatively below it toward ~0.10-0.14. The biggest correctness gap
             (word-segmented BM25 that collapses on CJK/Thai) is fixed for free at the tokenizer
             layer (BERT CJK basic-tokenizer) and superseded at the index layer (SPLADE/BGE
             learned-sparse on subword ids). The model-production gap (300-500ms python boot,
             torch dependency) is closed by Model2Vec static distillation, a sub-30MB
             lookup-table encoder with an official MIT Rust port, giving sovereign offline
             re-embedding. Everything maps to additive-only changes: new encoding IDs in the
             free 4-255 range, new optional sections at 0x09+, and new manifest/search_contract
             fields, with zero NEST_FORMAT_VERSION bump.

state of the art
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Convergent contract across the field: encoders emit L2-normalized vectors on the unit
hypersphere so cosine(a,b) == dot(a,b) (CLIP's logit_scale temperature, word2vec
normalize-at-load, every sentence-transformers Normalize module, Qwen3/BGE
truncate-then-renormalize). This single convention -- which nest must PIN in search_contract as
'normalize=l2' -- is the precondition that makes everything downstream cheap: pure SIMD dot
product with no per-query sqrt/div, and near-lossless int8 because every component lies in a
bounded [-1,1] with one symmetric per-vector scale. On top of that base, four independent
compression axes have matured: (A) VALUE PRECISION -- f16 (2x) and int8 (4x), both validated as
~lossless on normalized+PCA-whitened vectors (Model2Vec, sentence-transformers); nest already
ships these. (B) DIMENSIONALITY -- Matryoshka Representation Learning front-loads information so
vec[:d] is independently valid after renormalize; Qwen reports 4096->1024 keeps ~95% recall at
75% storage cut, ST reports 1024->256 at 0.6% drop; a pure deterministic prefix slice,
multiplicative with (A). (C) SUB-BYTE QUANTIZATION -- fastText product quantization (k=256
subvector k-means -> 1-byte codes, dsub=2 -> 1 byte per 2 dims = 8x base, norm decoupled,
asymmetric LUT scoring with no decode on the hot path) plus binary 1-bit quantization
(sign-threshold + popcount-Hamming, 32x, ST asymmetric rescore recovers ~96% recall). (D)
SPARSITY -- SPLADE (TEI) and BGE-M3 learned-sparse emit vocab-sized ~99%-zero vectors stored as
CSR (sorted term-id deltas + int8/f16 weights), an order of magnitude smaller than dense f32,
scored by integer merge-join, and zstd-compressible because they are off the float hot path. The
unifying retrieval discipline is compress-then-rescore: the lossy/sparse/ANN form does candidate
generation only, then a higher-fidelity rerank produces the final score (CLIP /rank, ST
asymmetric rescore, BGE weighted fusion, TEI cross-encoder) -- structurally identical to nest's
mandatory exact-cosine rerank after hnsw/bm25 RRF, which validates nest's design and means nest
can adopt ALL of these as candidate generators without weakening its 'returned score is real
cosine' guarantee. On the production side, the trend is away from heavy torch towards portable
artifacts: a model is 'an ONNX graph + a vocab' (clip-as-service) or, more radically, 'a [vocab
x dim] static lookup table' (Model2Vec: tokenize + gather + mean-pool, no forward pass, ~500x
faster CPU, 8-30MB, PCA-whitened + SIF/Zipf-weighted, int8-quantizable, with an official Rust
port needing only safetensors + tokenizers). Multilingual quality (the PT-BR/CJK/Thai concern)
is solved at two layers: BERT's CJK-aware basic tokenizer (insert boundaries around Han
codepoints, NFD accent strip, punctuation isolation) fixes lexical tokenization, and
M-CLIP/XLM-R/LaBSE text towers plus SPLADE/BGE subword sparsity fix it semantically.

top transferable innovations
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Product Quantization with asymmetric LUT scoring (fastText.zip): subvector k-means (k=256 ->
   1-byte codes, dsub=2) stores 1 byte per 2 dims (~8x base, ~16x vs f32) with norm decoupled;
   query builds a per-subvector lookup table once, then each candidate is dim/dsub byte-indexed
   loads + f32 adds with NO float decode on the hot path -- a perfect fit for nest's
   score-off-mmap SIMD model and the single highest-leverage embedding-compression steal.
   Demonstrated 250x model shrink (427MB->1.7MB) with negligible loss.
2. Matryoshka Representation Learning truncate-then-renormalize (Qwen3-Embedding, BGE-M3,
   sentence-transformers): embedding dimensionality becomes a near-lossless deterministic
   compression knob (slice prefix, L2-renormalize) that is multiplicative with int8/f16/PQ and
   also shrinks the HNSW index. 256-of-1024 at int8 = ~16x vs f32-1024. A pure deterministic
   slice, so byte-identical builds hold.
3. Compress-then-rescore as the universal search contract (sentence-transformers binary/int8,
   CLIP /rank, BGE fusion, TEI cross-encoder): store a lossy/sparse form for cheap candidate
   generation, rerank survivors at higher fidelity so the FINAL score is honest. This is nest's
   existing mandatory exact-cosine rerank generalized to the CODEC layer -- it is the ONLY safe
   way to compress embeddings while keeping 'returned score is real cosine', and it licenses
   adopting PQ, binary, and sparse all as candidate generators.
4. Learned sparse vectors (SPLADE in TEI, lexical_weights in BGE-M3): a vocab-sized ~99%-zero
   vector of (token_id, weight) pairs that is a semantic, language-agnostic, drop-in upgrade
   over BM25 tf-idf, stored as CSR (delta-coded ascending term ids + int8/f16 weights),
   zstd-compressed (off the float hot path), and scored by integer merge-join. Simultaneously
   shrinks the lexical index AND fixes nest's CJK/Thai BM25 gap with subword ids.
5. Model2Vec static distillation (MinishLab): collapse a sentence-transformer into a [vocab x
   dim] lookup table so encoding = tokenize + gather + mean-pool (no forward pass), PCA-whitened
   + SIF/Zipf-weighted offline, int8-quantizable, 8-30MB, ~500x faster CPU, dataset-free,
   deterministic, with an official MIT Rust port (safetensors + tokenizers). Gives nest a
   sovereign offline single-file embedder that kills the 300-500ms python boot and the torch
   dependency.
6. L2-normalize as a pinned build-time invariant (CLIP, word2vec, all ST Normalize heads): store
   embeddings pre-normalized so the SIMD dot equals true cosine with zero per-query sqrt/div,
   AND normalize-before-quantize tightens the int8 scale range to lift recall at the same byte
   cost. The precondition that makes every other compression axis safe to validate against a
   stable ground truth.
7. Binary quantization with popcount-Hamming first stage (sentence-transformers): sign-threshold
   normalized f32 to 1 bit/dim (32x), packbits, retrieve by XOR + popcount (~2 CPU cycles, a
   tiny AVX2 popcnt / NEON vcnt kernel), then asymmetric rescore against int8/f32 recovers ~96%
   recall. The cheapest possible first-stage candidate gate, fully inside nest's SIMD
   dispatcher.
8. BERT CJK-aware basic tokenizer + WordPiece greedy-longest-match: a data-only, model-free,
   deterministic pure function (insert boundaries around Han/CJK codepoint ranges, NFD accent
   strip, punctuation isolation, then greedy ## subword match over an ordered vocab) that fixes
   nest's named CJK/Thai BM25 degradation with ZERO bm25 codec change -- terms stay UTF-8
   length-prefixed alphabetical, section 0x08 byte-identical.
9. PCA-whitening before quantization (Model2Vec, sentence-transformers): decorrelate/normalize
   the embedding space offline (a build-time matrix multiply) so int8/binary/PQ lose ~nothing;
   ~2.8% gain even at full dim. A pure build-side conditioning pass that strengthens every nest
   codec at the same byte cost.
10. Contrastive distillation to a smaller-dimension student (SetFit, BGE self-distillation):
   re-embed a corpus into a lower-dim space (768->256) whose neighbor ordering is preserved,
   attacking the float COUNT (not just width) -- the one axis zstd/int8 cannot touch --
   multiplicative with int8, guarded by exact rerank. A 'nano' preset below tiny.

nest recommendations
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Add Matryoshka dimension-truncation as a build-time codec: nest-python slices each f32 vector
  to a chosen mrl_dim prefix and re-L2-normalizes before writing; store full_dim + mrl_dim in
  the embeddings section header and mirror into search_contract. Runtime needs only honor the
  smaller per-vector stride from the manifest -- existing AVX2/NEON/scalar kernels and the int8
  view run unchanged on the shorter row. Exact rerank stays true cosine because vectors are
  renormalized on the prefix.
    where:  build: nest-python embedding path (truncate+renormalize); format: new manifest field
            full_dim/mrl_dim + crates/nest-format/src/sections/contract.rs SearchContract (add
            mrl_dim, full_dim as optional fields); runtime: crates/nest-runtime/src/simd/mod.rs
            already strides by dim, no kernel change; nest-cli stats surfaces the size/recall
            tradeoff.
    impact: high
    effort: low
    from:   https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-Embedding, https://github.com/FlagOpen/FlagEmbedding,
            https://github.com/UKPLab/sentence-transformers

* Add encoding=4 'pq' (product quantization) as a new embeddings codec in the free 4-255
  additive range, sibling to int8.rs. On-disk layout mirrors int8.rs prefix conventions: u32
  payload_version, u32 dsub, seeded-k-means codebook (m_subspaces*256*dsub f32 centroids),
  per-vector scalar norm (reuse int8 norm machinery), then n*(dim/dsub) u8 codes. Runtime SIMD
  dispatcher builds the asymmetric query LUT (query dotted vs mmapped codebook via existing dot
  kernels) once per query, scores each candidate as dim/dsub byte-indexed adds off mmap, then
  feeds the mandatory exact-cosine rerank -- PQ is only the ANN coarse filter, never the final
  score. Enables a 'nano' preset (~0.14 ratio) below tiny. k-means MUST be seeded in the python
  builder for byte-identical builds.
    where:  format: crates/nest-format/src/encoding/pq.rs (new) registered in encoding/mod.rs and
            writer/encoding_choice.rs, encoding id 4 in layout/mod.rs; runtime:
            crates/nest-runtime/src/simd/ new PQ scoring path +
            crates/nest-runtime/src/search.rs candidate feed into existing rerank; build: seeded
            k-means + optional greedy coverage pruning in nest-python.
    impact: high
    effort: high
    from:   https://github.com/facebookresearch/fastText

* Add an optional learned-sparse index section at 0x09 (reserved range, additive) storing
  per-chunk ascending (token_id:u32 delta-varint, weight:int8/f16) CSR arrays; zstd the section
  (it is scored by integer merge-join, NOT a SIMD dot off mmap, so zstd is safe here unlike
  embeddings). Generalize the existing Bm25Index into a SparseIndex whose postings hold learned
  weights; s_lex = sum w_q*w_d via merge-join replaces tf-idf BM25 while reusing deterministic
  alphabetical/ascending ordering and the existing reciprocal-rank fusion. nest-python computes
  SPLADE/BGE-M3 lexical_weights at build time; rust does only integer merge-join at serve time
  (stays offline). Candidate generation only; exact-cosine rerank stays ground truth. Replaces
  the word-segmented BM25 (fixes CJK/Thai) AND shrinks the lexical index.
    where:  format: new section 0x09 in layout/mod.rs + crates/nest-format/src/sections/ (CSR codec,
            zstd-eligible); runtime: crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/ generalized to SparseIndex
            (index.rs, codec.rs) reusing fusion.rs RRF; build: nest-python
            SPLADE/lexical_weights step; search_contract gains a fusion descriptor.
    impact: high
    effort: high
    from:   https://github.com/huggingface/text-embeddings-inference,
            https://github.com/FlagOpen/FlagEmbedding

* Pin 'normalize=l2' as a checked invariant in search_contract and document/enforce
  L2-normalized embeddings: nest-python L2-normalizes (and for int8, normalizes BEFORE
  quantizing to tighten the per-vector scale range). Runtime reads normalize=l2 and runs a pure
  dot product with zero per-query sqrt/div; the exact rerank becomes a bare FMA scan. This is
  the precondition that makes int8/PQ/binary/MRL all safe and validates the 0.992 recall claim
  against a stable ground truth.
    where:  format: crates/nest-format/src/sections/contract.rs (normalize already a field --
            enforce/cross-check at open in reader); build: nest-python normalize-then-quantize

            norm when normalize=l2.
    impact: high
    effort: low
    from:   https://github.com/openai/CLIP, https://github.com/tmikolov/word2vec,
            https://github.com/UKPLab/sentence-transformers,
            https://github.com/cpcdoy/rust-sbert

    where:  build: nest-python static-embed mode + model fingerprint in provenance; optional runtime:

    where:  build: nest-python whitening pass (numpy, build-side only) before

    impact: medium
    effort: medium

* Add a binary (1-bit) shadow codec as a new reserved encoding id for first-stage candidate

  vectors. Recovers ~96% recall via asymmetric rescore; never touches Python; the binary form is

    impact: medium

* Steal GloVe's external-memory sorted-run + min-heap k-way-merge-with-accumulation for building
  large index sections (bm25/sparse postings, future graph edges) on a minimal-RAM budget:

compression wins
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Product quantization (encoding=4, dsub=2, k=256) stores 1 byte per 2 dims = ~8x over raw f32
    and ~2x over the current int8 codec, scored off mmap via asymmetric LUT with no float decode
    and guarded by exact rerank -- the single biggest lever to push embeddings (the largest,
    non-zstd-compressible section) toward a ~0.14 ratio and beat int8's 0.283 tiny preset.
    fastText.zip evidence: 427MB->1.7MB (250x) at negligible loss.
  - Matryoshka dimension truncation is multiplicative with value-precision: 256-of-1024 prefix
    (4x on dims) at int8 (4x on width) = ~16x smaller embeddings than f32-1024, a pure
    deterministic prefix slice with ~0.6-5% recall cost, also shrinking the HNSW index footprint
    since graph vectors are shorter. The one compression axis (float COUNT) that zstd/f16/int8
    cannot touch.
  - Learned-sparse (SPLADE/BGE) CSR sections are ~99% zeros: stored as delta-varint ascending
    term ids + int8/f16 weights then zstd'd (safe because off the SIMD hot path), they carry
    semantic keyword search in an order of magnitude fewer bytes than dense f32 or a raw text
    index -- a text-dominant corpus can ship retrieval quality near dense at a fraction of the
    bytes, advancing compress-and-still-search.
  - Binary 1-bit quantization is 32x on the embeddings section (1024d -> 128 bytes) as a
    first-stage candidate gate; asymmetric rescore against the int8/f32 vectors recovers ~96%
    recall, so the 32x form is used only for candidate generation and the final score stays real
    cosine.
  - PCA-whitening + L2-normalization BEFORE quantization is a free quality multiplier: it
    conditions the space so int8/binary/PQ lose ~nothing (Model2Vec reaches 25% of original size
    with no measurable loss; ~2.8% gain even at full dim), letting nest push to lower-bit
    packing at the SAME byte cost and lifting recall at the 0.283 tiny ratio.
  - Contrastive distillation to a smaller-dimension student (768->256) attacks the float COUNT
    directly and composes multiplicatively with int8 (~3x on top of ~4x), plausibly pushing a
    'nano' preset below the current 0.283 tiny ratio, with the exact-cosine rerank guarding
    recall against the lossier embedder.
  - Codebook + residual via topic/IVF centroids (Top2Vec geometry): store K centroids +
    per-chunk topic_id (1-2 bytes) + small residual, exploiting redundancy ACROSS chunks;
    reordering chunks by topic_id also places similar embeddings adjacent, improving
    byte-locality and zstd ratio on the zstd-eligible sections (chunks_canonical, provenance).
    Auto-generated label terms double as a zstd custom dictionary seed for chunks_canonical.
  - Frequency-rank id remapping (GloVe/word2vec/Model2Vec Zipf) on bm25/sparse postings clusters
    dense entries at small ids, directly improving zstd entropy coding on the non-embedding
    index sections at zero quality cost.

watch-outs (conflict with nest constraints)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - NEVER zstd the embeddings section: it is scored directly from mmap on the SIMD hot path, so
    any whole-section entropy coding breaks score-off-disk. PQ/binary/MRL/int8 are fine because
    they are FIXED-stride codecs read by stride, not stream-decompressed. Sparse/CSR sections
    (0x09) and centroid/topic sections ARE zstd-eligible precisely because they are scored by
    merge-join, not a float dot off mmap -- keep that boundary sharp.
  - Do NOT vendor torch/libtorch (rust-sbert tch-rs, rust-bert) or candle/ONNX-Runtime servers
    into the small rust core: these are hundreds-of-MB native deps that violate
    single-file/offline/small-core. Inference stays build-side in nest-python; the runtime only
    consumes floats. The ONLY runtime-embeddable encoder is Model2Vec (model2vec-rs: safetensors
    + tokenizers, MIT), and even that should be a gated feature, not core.
  - k-means (PQ codebook), PCA rotation, HDBSCAN/UMAP clustering, and any sampling shuffle are
    nondeterministic by default and will break byte-identical builds. PQ k-means and topic
    clustering MUST be seeded; UMAP/HDBSCAN (Numba/SciPy, fragile, non-deterministic) must stay
    python build-side and never enter the rust runtime -- use seeded k-means or fixed-threshold
    greedy cosine agglomeration for any in-runtime clustering. GloVe's default rand_long()
    shuffle must be reseeded if adapted.
  - Adopting a learned subword/WordPiece/SPLADE vocab changes the BM25/sparse term space, so
    determinism requires the vocab be FROZEN and its fingerprint recorded in
    provenance/search_contract; the greedy-match algorithm and CJK ranges are clean-room
    reimplementable, but a shipped vocab.txt artifact bloats the single file and is
    training-produced -- prefer the model-free CJK basic tokenizer as the must-have and treat
    WordPiece/SPLADE vocab as optional.
  - Honor the additive-only v1 constraint precisely: PQ/binary go in encoding ids 4-255 (4 is
    the next free), sparse/topic/splade go in section ids 0x09+; new manifest/search_contract
    fields are fine, but do NOT change the meaning of any existing field (mrl_dim/full_dim must
    be additive optional, not a redefinition of dim) or you force a true v2 NEST_FORMAT_VERSION
    bump.
  - Keep every new rust module single-responsibility and <=300 lines (333 hard cap): PQ codec,
    popcount-Hamming kernel, SparseIndex, external_merge, and CJK tokenizer each need their own
    file; do not fold PQ scoring into the existing simd/mod.rs (already ~144 lines) past the
    cap.
  - ColBERT late-interaction (BGE-M3 multi-vector MaxSim) is O(N_q*N_d*dim) and storage-heavy
    (per-token vectors) -- use it only as an optional rerank-only signal if ever, NEVER as a
    candidate generator or a default, to keep the runtime light; the practical hybrid is dense +
    learned-sparse.
  - Never weaken the mandatory exact-cosine rerank: PQ, binary, sparse, and distilled-student
    vectors are ALL lossy and must remain candidate generators only. The returned score must
    always be real cosine recomputed against the highest-fidelity stored vectors, or nest loses
    its 'score is real cosine' guarantee.
  - logit_scale temperature scaling (CLIP) and weighted-sum fusion (BGE) introduce calibration
    scalars that, if folded into the stored/returned score, would make scores model-dependent
    and break cross-encoding score stability -- keep them as an OPTIONAL self-describing scoring
    mode in search_contract, never the default, so the default returned score stays plain
    cosine.
  - Image/CLIP support must not introduce a per-modality SIMD branch or a separate image codec:
    only L2-normalized 512-d vectors in the shared embeddings section qualify (they
    cosine-search identically to text). Anything requiring a distinct distance or layout would
    fork the hot path and violate the minimal-format goal.
