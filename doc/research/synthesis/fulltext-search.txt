================================================================================================
synthesis :: fulltext-search
full-text and lexical search engines (inverted index, BM25, edge cases)
================================================================================================

state of the art and concrete nest recommendations for this category.

summary:     Across 16 lexical engines (Lucene, Tantivy/Quickwit/Toshi/Summa, Meilisearch, Typesense,
             Manticore/MCL, RediSearch, Xapian, SeekStorm, Sonic, tinysearch, Stork, FlexSearch,
             Michael-JB/bm25) the field has converged on one canonical, near-entropy posting
             codec and one canonical term-dictionary structure, both of which nest is currently
             NOT using. nest's bm25 section (0x08) today (verified in
             crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/codec.rs:14-39) writes every posting as a raw fixed 8
             bytes (u32 doc + u32 tf), raw u32 doc_lengths (4 bytes/doc), and length-prefixed
             UTF-8 terms with a 4-byte length prefix each; index.rs::search (lines 101-115) does
             exhaustive term-at-a-time OR touching every posting. This is the single
             least-compressed, least-optimized section in the whole container and is squarely on
             the 'compress more than everyone' critical path for the hybrid and tiny presets.
             The state of the art replaces all three layers losslessly and deterministically:
             (1) postings = delta-gap doc ids + per-128-block minimal-bit-width bitpacking
             (FOR/PForDelta/BP128) with a varint tail, typically collapsing 8 bytes/posting to
             1-2 bytes; (2) term dictionary = an FST (shared prefix AND suffix minimization,
             BurntSushi `fst` crate, MIT/Unlicense) that also unlocks prefix/range/typo lookup
             for free; (3) doc lengths = 1-byte log-quantized fieldnorm or a min+bitpacked
             column. Query-side, Block-Max WAND / MaxScore turns the O(all postings) OR into
             early-terminating top-k by storing per-block max-impact bounds. Every one of these
             is a pure internal payload change inside the already-reserved 0x08 section gated by
             BM25_PAYLOAD_VERSION (currently 1 in index.rs:8), needs NO NEST_FORMAT_VERSION
             bump, stays deterministic (terms already sorted alphabetically, bit-widths are pure
             functions of the data), decodes branch-free off mmap (matching nest's embedding
             hot-path discipline), and is recall-safe because BM25 is only a candidate generator
             before nest's mandatory exact-cosine rerank.

state of the art
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The settled lexical-index stack is: FST term dictionary (term -> ordinal, mmap zero-copy,
O(term-len) lookup, free prefix/range/Levenshtein scan) over 128-doc posting BLOCKS where doc
ids are delta-encoded then bitpacked at each block's local minimal bit-width
b=ceil(log2(max_gap+1)), term-freqs bitpacked in a parallel block stream, with a VInt/varint
remainder for the trailing partial block and rare terms (df<128). A per-block skip list
(last_doc_id, byte offset, max_term_freq, fieldnorm bound) drives Block-Max WAND / MaxScore
dynamic top-k pruning so query cost scales with surviving blocks, not corpus size (production
reports ~10-20% of exhaustive-OR time). Doc lengths compress to 1 byte via BM25
length-saturation (log-quantized fieldnorm). Roaring bitmaps (array/bitmap/run containers per
64k chunk, ~1.25 bits/doc dense) are the alternative posting encoding when SIMD set-intersection
on the compressed form matters (SeekStorm, Meilisearch). The whole index is immutable,
self-describing, byte-range addressable and served straight off mmap with no decompression of
the whole section (Quickwit/Summa prove block-addressable lexical search; Sonic proves mmap'd
FST vocab at a few MB RAM). bitpacking decode hits 5-7 billion int/s SIMD with a clean scalar
fallback (the `bitpacking` crate, Tantivy's subcrate, MIT). Manticore/MCL adds per-block
ADAPTIVE codec selection (const / dictionary-when-<256-uniques / delta+bitpack / raw, chosen by
cheapest cost per block) plus min-max zone maps for skip-without-decode. The frontier extremes:
tinysearch's per-document Xor8/BinaryFuse8 filters (~9 bits/unique-token, no postings at all)
for an ultra-tiny lexical channel, and FlexSearch's build-time ordinal-bucket pre-scoring (score
becomes a 4-bit ordinal, not an f32). All the reusable building blocks are permissively licensed
(Tantivy/fst/bitpacking/roaring/xorf all MIT or Apache); the GPL/AGPL/SSPL daemons (Typesense,
Xapian, Manticore daemon, Quickwit, Summa, RediSearch) are reference-only but their algorithms
are public-domain IR and safe to clean-room in Rust.

top transferable innovations
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Delta-gap + per-128-block minimal-bit-width bitpacking of doc ids with varint tail
   (FOR/PForDelta/BP128) - the universal posting codec, 4-8x lossless shrink of nest's raw
   8-byte postings, deterministic, branch-free mmap decode
2. FST term dictionary (BurntSushi fst, MIT/Unlicense) - shared prefix+suffix minimization
   compresses vocab and unlocks prefix/range/Levenshtein lookup, closing the typo and CJK/Thai
   gaps
3. Block-Max WAND / MaxScore top-k pruning - turns the exhaustive term-at-a-time OR into
   early-terminating DAAT, recall-safe before the mandatory exact-cosine rerank
4. 1-byte log-quantized fieldnorm - 4x shrink of the raw u32 doc_lengths array, lossless-enough
   under BM25 saturation
5. Separated doc-length stream + sorted-term front-coding - near-free dictionary shrink and a
   simpler stepping stone before a full FST
6. Per-block adaptive codec selection (const/dict/delta-bitpack/raw) + min-max zone maps (MCL) -
   exploits local uniformity zstd misses, reusable across all integer-id sections
7. Per-document Xor8/BinaryFuse8 membership filter (~9 bits/unique-token) - a kilobyte-scale
   lexical channel for a tiny+lexical preset, false positives killed by rerank
8. Build-time ordinal-bucket pre-scoring (FlexSearch) - tf becomes a 4-bit ordinal, doc ids
   group into delta-friendly runs, replaces per-posting float math
9. Roaring-bitmap posting lists with SIMD set-ops on the compressed form - near-optimal for
   sparse-to-dense doc-id sets, reuses the AVX2/NEON dispatcher
10. BM25-as-sparse-embedding (Michael-JB/bm25) - scoring becomes a sparse dot product reusing
   nest's SIMD dispatcher exactly like the dense cosine path

nest recommendations
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Replace raw (u32 doc, u32 tf) postings with delta-gap doc ids + per-128-doc-block
  minimal-bit-width bitpacking + a varint tail; tf as a parallel bitpacked block. Postings are
  already doc-sorted (index.rs:69), so gaps are positive and small. Bump BM25_PAYLOAD_VERSION
  1->2 ONLY (index.rs:8); no NEST_FORMAT_VERSION change. Keep the decode kernel scalar
  (word-at-a-time shifts) to honor portability/never-panic, with an optional AVX2/NEON unpack
  later via the existing simd dispatcher.
    where:  crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/codec.rs (rewrite to_bytes/from_bytes postings loop, lines 33-37
            and 72-77); put the bitpack/unpack primitive in a new
            crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/bitpack.rs (~80-120 LOC, under the 300-line cap);
            update the layout doc-comment in bm25/mod.rs:10-28.
    impact: Highest single win. Collapses the dominant posting array from 8 bytes/posting to ~1-2 bytes
            (4-8x) on the PT-BR corpus, shrinking section 0x08 and the hybrid/tiny presets
            several-fold with zero recall loss. Bitpacked ints are near entropy-optimal so it
            composes with (does not fight) the existing zstd-on-non-embedding pass.
    effort: medium
    from:   apache/lucene, quickwit-oss/tantivy, quickwit-oss/quickwit, toshi-search/Toshi,
            typesense/typesense

* Quantize doc_lengths from raw u32 to a 1-byte log-quantized fieldnorm (BM25 length-saturation
  makes the loss negligible and the exact-cosine rerank absorbs it), stored as a flat
  little-endian byte column. Optionally generalize to a min+num_bits bitpacked column for an
  exact-but-compact alternative.
    where:  crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/codec.rs (doc_lengths write at lines 22-24 and read at 55-58);
            decode the quantized length back in index.rs::search where dl is read (line 109).
            Document the quantization table in bm25/mod.rs.
    impact: 4x shrink of the per-doc length array (4 bytes -> 1 byte) with no measurable ranking change
            after rerank. Small but free and clean.
    effort: low
    from:   quickwit-oss/quickwit, toshi-search/Toshi, apache/lucene

* Add Block-Max WAND / MaxScore top-k pruning: store per-128-block max_term_freq (and a
  doc-length-derived BM25 upper bound) in the posting skip header, then make search iterate
  Document-at-a-Time skipping blocks that cannot enter the top-k heap, instead of the current
  exhaustive term-at-a-time OR over every posting.
    where:  new crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/wand.rs (keep WAND out of index.rs to stay under 300 lines, as
            the extracts suggest); rewrite the scoring loop in
            crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/index.rs::search (lines 101-115). Block metadata is
            written in codec.rs alongside the bitpacked blocks.
    impact: Cuts search-text query work to a fraction of corpus size, directly attacking the known latency
            gap. Recall-safe: BM25 only generates candidates for rrf_union (fusion.rs) before
            the mandatory exact-cosine rerank, so skipping/reordering candidates never changes
            returned scores.
    effort: medium
    from:   apache/lucene, quickwit-oss/tantivy, SeekStorm/SeekStorm, izihawa/summa

* Replace the length-prefixed alphabetical term blob with an FST (BurntSushi `fst` crate,
  MIT/Unlicense, pure-Rust, no_std-friendly) built from the already-sorted terms, mapping term
  -> u32 term-ordinal. Deterministic by construction over sorted input. Stage it AFTER the
  postings codec so the two land independently.
    where:  crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/codec.rs term loop (write lines 28-32, read 60-70) and the terms
            HashMap in index.rs (lines 32, 59, 78); add `fst` to crates/nest-runtime/Cargo.toml.
            Consider a separate bm25/dict.rs module to respect the file-size cap and
            single-responsibility rule.
    impact: Compresses the vocabulary via shared prefix+suffix (beats front-coding) and unlocks
            prefix/range/typo lookup for free, closing nest's typo-tolerance and CJK/Thai gaps.
            Only new dependency in the set, but it is the canonical, vetted, permissive choice
            used by 5 of the 16 engines.
    effort: medium
    from:   meilisearch/meilisearch, quickwit-oss/tantivy, izihawa/summa, valeriansaliou/sonic,
            toshi-search/Toshi

* Before (or instead of) the FST dependency, front-code the alphabetical term dictionary: store
  shared-prefix-length-with-previous-term + suffix bytes instead of a full 4-byte length prefix
  + full UTF-8 per term. Nearly free since terms are already sorted, zero new dependency.
    where:  crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/codec.rs term write (lines 28-32) and read (lines 60-70).
    impact: Meaningful dictionary shrink on natural-language PT-BR vocab with no new crate; a clean stepping
            stone that de-risks the FST decision and is reversible.
    effort: low
    from:   apache/lucene, github.com/xapian/xapian-docsprint

* Generalize the bitpack primitive into a reusable per-block adaptive integer codec (const /
  dictionary-when-<256-uniques / delta+bitpack / raw, chosen per block by cheapest cost, with a
  tiny repr(C) LE min-max header) as a NEW additive encoding id in the reserved 4-255 range,
  applied to non-embedding integer sections only (postings, chunk_ids, spans), NEVER embeddings.
    where:  crates/nest-format/src/encoding/ (new module, mirror the int8.rs/float16.rs/zstd_codec.rs
            pattern; register an id beside SECTION_ENCODING_* in layout/mod.rs:59-62) and wire
            into writer/encoding_choice.rs SectionEncoding enum; decode in
            encoding/mod.rs::decode_payload (lines 44-55). Min-max zone maps enable
            skip-without-decode in the bm25 search path.
    impact: Exploits local uniformity that a single global zstd pass misses (const/dict blocks collapse to a
            few bytes), and becomes the shared primitive for every integer-id section in the
            container, advancing compress-more-than-everyone beyond just bm25.
    effort: high
    from:   manticoresoftware/manticoresearch (+ manticoresoftware/columnar, the MCL library),
            RediSearch/RediSearch, quickwit-oss/tantivy

* Add an optional ultra-compact 'membership-only' bm25 codec variant: a deterministic per-chunk
  Xor8/BinaryFuse8 filter (xorf crate, MIT/Apache) over the chunk token set, stored as flat
  little-endian arrays one filter per chunk_id, probed in place off mmap with zero
  deserialization. Used as a cheap candidate generator (AND query terms, score by hit count)
  feeding RRF; the exact-cosine rerank removes the ~0.39% false positives.
    where:  a new codec variant selected inside section 0x08 (gated by BM25_PAYLOAD_VERSION) in
            crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/; build-side filter construction in nest-python (O(n)
            peeling is build-time only). Feeds the existing fusion.rs rrf_union path.
    impact: Enables a 'tiny+lexical' preset where the keyword channel costs only ~9 bits/unique-token/chunk
            (kilobytes total) instead of full postings, keeping lexical search alive in the
            smallest preset. Lossy candidate-gen only, never leaks into returned scores.
    effort: medium
    from:   tinysearch/tinysearch

* Upgrade the tokenizer into a swappable pipeline with deterministic normalization presets (raw
  -> diacritic-fold -> Snowball stem -> optional CJK/Thai n-gram/bigram segmentation), keeping
  the current behavior as the default preset so builds stay byte-reproducible. Pair with FST
  Levenshtein lookup for typo tolerance. The exact-cosine rerank means tokenizer changes only
  affect candidate recall, not final scores.
    where:  crates/nest-runtime/src/bm25/tokenize.rs (currently a single fixed function, lines 8-28);
            introduce a Tokenizer trait shape and presets while preserving the default. Both
            build (index.rs:42) and query (index.rs:97) must use the identical preset for
            determinism.
    impact: Closes the documented CJK/Thai word-segmentation gap and adds typo/prefix search; FST-driven
            normalization also collapses surface forms, shrinking the dictionary. Must be opt-in
            per preset to preserve byte-identical reproducible builds.
    effort: medium
    from:   meilisearch/meilisearch, nextapps-de/flexsearch, Michael-JB/bm25, typesense/typesense

compression wins
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Postings: delta-gap doc ids + per-128-block minimal-bit-width bitpacking + varint tail
    replaces raw 8 bytes/posting (u32 doc + u32 tf) -> ~1-2 bytes/posting, a 4-8x structural
    shrink of the dominant array in section 0x08. Near entropy-optimal so it stacks with the
    existing zstd-on-non-embedding pass for residual gains.
  - tf stream: most term frequencies are 1-2, so a parallel bitpacked tf block needs ~2-4 bits
    each vs the current full 4-byte u32 (8-16x on the tf column alone).
  - Doc lengths: 1-byte log-quantized fieldnorm replaces raw u32 doc_lengths -> 4x shrink (4
    bytes -> 1 byte per doc), lossless-enough under BM25 length saturation and fully absorbed by
    the exact-cosine rerank.
  - Term dictionary: FST (shared prefix+suffix minimization) or front-coding (shared-prefix-len
    + suffix) replaces full 4-byte-length-prefixed UTF-8 per term -> large vocabulary shrink on
    natural-language PT-BR terms, with FST also delivering prefix/typo lookup for free.
  - Per-block adaptive codec (const / dict<256 / delta+bitpack / raw) collapses locally-uniform
    integer regions to a handful of bytes that a single global zstd pass misses, applicable to
    postings, chunk_ids, spans, and future HNSW edge lists alike.
  - Ordinal-bucket pre-scoring turns the per-posting tf into a 4-bit ordinal and groups doc ids
    into monotonic delta-friendly runs, which then zstd-compress far better than interleaved
    (doc,tf) pairs.
  - Xor8/BinaryFuse8 per-chunk filters give a whole lexical channel at a fixed ~9
    bits/unique-token with no postings/varints, an order of magnitude under full BM25, enabling
    a tiny+lexical preset measured in kilobytes.
  - Roaring bitmap posting lists (array/bitmap/run containers, ~1.25 bits/doc dense) are
    near-optimal for sparse-to-dense doc-id sets and still SIMD-intersectable on the compressed
    form, an alternative size win for dense terms. Net effect: section 0x08, today the
    least-compressed section in the container, becomes one of the most-compressed, advancing the
    compress-more-than-everyone north star specifically on the hybrid and tiny presets.

watch-outs (conflict with nest constraints)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - SeekStorm's sharding makes BM25 scores non-deterministic and non-comparable across shards
    (per-shard avgdl, df, posting lengths, load-balanced non-deterministically). This would
    break nest's byte-identical reproducible builds and global-stats ranking. Steal only the
    encoding/pruning/SIMD-set-ops; keep a single deterministic non-sharded index with global
    stats and alphabetical term order.
  - License hygiene: Typesense (GPL-3.0), Xapian (GPL-2.0+), Manticore daemon (GPL-3.0),
    Quickwit (AGPL-3.0), Summa (AGPL-3.0), RediSearch (RSALv2/SSPLv1, not OSI-open) must NOT be
    vendored or copied. Their algorithms (FOR, delta+varint, BMW, range trees, trie-Levenshtein)
    are public IR and safe to clean-room in Rust. Only depend on the permissive building blocks:
    fst (MIT/Unlicense), bitpacking (MIT), roaring-rs (Apache/MIT), xorf (MIT/Apache), the
    Michael-JB bm25 ideas (MIT).
  - Any FST or quantization change to section 0x08 must stay an INTERNAL payload change gated by
    BM25_PAYLOAD_VERSION (index.rs:8, currently 1), NOT a NEST_FORMAT_VERSION bump: 0x08 is
    optional/additive and its section meaning is unchanged. Bumping the frozen format version
    for an internal codec swap would violate the additive-only v1 rule.
  - Determinism must be preserved end to end: bit-widths must be a pure function of the sorted
    data, FST built over already-alphabetical terms (canonical), bucket boundaries fixed,
    hashing seeded. Do NOT adopt hash-based token IDs (Michael-JB/bm25, Sonic) for the canonical
    dictionary: collisions plus non-stable ordering break byte-reproducibility and reopenable
    nest:// citations. Keep an explicit sorted term dictionary.
  - Lossy tricks (fieldnorm quantization, ordinal-bucket scores, Xor-filter false positives,
    tokenizer normalization changes) are only safe because BM25 is a candidate generator before
    the mandatory exact-cosine rerank. They must NEVER leak into the returned score and must NOT
    touch the embedding hot path (embeddings stay raw f32/f16/int8 for direct SIMD scoring per
    nest's codec rules). Do not apply stream/columnar compression to embeddings.
  - SIMD bitpacking is optional, not required: ship a scalar word-at-a-time unpack first
    (matches the existing avx2/neon/scalar dispatcher and the never-panic + portability
    constraints), add vectorized unpack later. Do not introduce a hard dependency on a crate
    whose scalar fallback is unacceptable or which can panic in library code.
  - Skip the remote/IPFS/network Directory ideas (Summa, Quickwit object-store, Sonic RocksDB,
    SeekStorm RAM-vs-mmap server) and the 32KB-chunk HTTP caching: nest is
    offline-by-construction and never opens a socket; the OS page cache already demand-pages the
    mmapped file. Borrow only the block-addressable on-disk layout philosophy.
  - Respect file hygiene: keep the block codec in codec.rs/bitpack.rs, WAND in wand.rs, FST dict
    in dict.rs, each single-responsibility and under the 300-line cap (333 hard). Do NOT adopt
    rmp-serde/MessagePack (Stork) or Toshi/Summa server frontends: nest's existing repr(C) LE +
    zstd-on-non-embedding already beats raw MessagePack and the server layers are out of scope.
  - FlexSearch/Stork/tinysearch are in-memory or WASM-heap-resident designs that get slow above
    ~1MB corpora or on short queries; borrow their build-time-precompute and ordinal/filter size
    tricks, not their resident-memory query model. nest's mmap-and-skip discipline is already
    superior.
